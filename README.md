Group Activity Recognition: A Hierarchical Deep Temporal ModelWelcome to the Group Activity Recognition project! This repository is dedicated to solving the complex problem of understanding what a group of people is doing collectively, based on the actions of individual members over time.Whether you are a beginner in Computer Vision or an experienced researcher, this guide will walk you through the core concepts, the dataset, the model architecture, and the historical results that form the foundation of this work.🧠 1. The Problem: What is Group Activity Recognition?In traditional action recognition, the goal is often to figure out what a single person is doing (e.g., "running", "jumping"). However, in real-world scenarios like sports, security, or social gatherings, people act as a group.The Challenge: How do we teach an AI to understand the overall activity of a scene (e.g., a "Left Spike" in volleyball) when that activity depends on the synchronized actions of 12 different players (e.g., one jumping, two blocking, others waiting)?The Solution: We infer the temporal dynamics of the whole activity based on the dynamics of the individual people. This project implements a Hierarchical Deep Temporal Model inspired by the seminal CVPR 2016 paper by Mostafa S. Ibrahim et al. It uses a two-stage approach:Understand what each person is doing individually over time.Combine this information to understand what the whole team is doing.🏐 2. Model Architecture (Baseline 8 Focus)This repository focuses heavily on implementing and understanding a Two-stage Hierarchical Model (Baseline 8). This architecture is designed to integrate individual player features and team-level dynamics over time.The Two-Stage ConceptThe model is broken down into two main classifiers acting sequentially:Stage 1: Player Activity Temporal Classifier (Person Dynamics)We need to track how individual players move over short clips (sequences of frames).Spatial Extraction: We crop out each player using their bounding boxes and pass them through a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) like ResNet to extract visual features.Temporal Modeling: An LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) network processes the sequence of these features for each player individually. This captures their unique temporal dependencies (e.g., a player crouching, then jumping).Stage 2: Group Activity Temporal Classifier (Team Dynamics)Now we must combine the individual insights to figure out the team's strategy.Team Independent Pooling: A critical feature of Baseline 8 is how it groups players. Instead of mixing all 12 players together (which destroys geometric context and confuses right vs. left teams), it applies an adaptive max-pooling operation to each team separately (e.g., Team 1: players 1-6, Team 2: players 7-12).Concatenation: The pooled features from the two teams are then combined.Group LSTM: A second, higher-level LSTM processes these combined team features over time to classify the final group activity.

Historical Context & Original ResultsThis project pays homage to the original CVPR 2016 paper. To appreciate the model's architecture, it is helpful to look at the baseline progressions established by the original authors.They tested multiple architectures, starting from simple single-frame image classification (B1) all the way to the 2-stage Hierarchical Model (which corresponds to Baseline 8 in modern implementations).Key takeaway from the original research: Capturing temporal dynamics (LSTM) and treating the team spatial arrangements carefully (Team Pooling) significantly boosted accuracy.As shown above, the original Two-stage Hierarchical Model achieved an impressive 81.9% accuracy on the dataset, proving the effectiveness of the two-stage LSTM approach.📚 Acknowledgments & ReferencesThis repository is heavily inspired by the original work:A Hierarchical Deep Temporal Model for Group Activity Recognition.
